### Exercise

Using the community maturation indicator, answer these questions about your community.

**Use this space to take notes if miro isn’t accessible**

✍️ What is the desired/necessary level of participation in your community and why?

✍️  What is the actual level of participation in your community?

✍️ What stage of community building are you at for your desired/actual level of participation?

✍️ Is this what you had expected? If not, what is missing in your plan?

✍️ What actions could you take next?

**Use this space to take notes if miro isn’t accessible**

✍️ What *could* you do next => Think about cost vs impact

✍️ What are your goals for September, December, March 2026

✍️ Prompts for thinking about delivery


* What resources (including training) will you require to deliver each goal?
* Who will be accountable for delivery?
* How will I communicate this goal?
* How will I know when I’ve been successful? 
* Does delivery require changes to existing workflows, policies, or organisational structures?
* What are the biggest risks to failure?
* How can these risks be mitigated?

    * How to manage/reengage with 2 different communities that have overlapping interests but different objectives

    * How to leverage the more active community but maintaining distinction to avoid confusion with the comms channels (e.g. separate newsletters)
* Who in your project is involved in addressing it?
    * Me (Community Manager) and the Community Lead
* How will solving this issue impact your community?
    * Give existing members more content they’d like to engage with
    * Attract more members
* Discussion I’ve like to have:
- [x] Solutions/Advice

* Discussion
    * We know there are people interested in this space, passive 1-2 years
    * Existing community around dry-labs(?) certification
        * Forum

    * Easier to engage with the smaller community as it’s clearer what news they want
    * Know I need to write the strategy, mission statement
    * Varying levels of interest

    * Mission makes it clear what to expect from certain parts of the community
        * It’s ok to know that there are sub-sectors

        * Otherwise you have to talk a lot and negotiate
    * Q: have you seen any other communities which look aspirational?
        * Other green computing spaces
        * What can we do better than others
        * SSI community as a model
    * Community members are having to clearly delineate when they’re having Green DiSC conversations vs. Leaf 
    * Get out and speak to the community members to understand how they think/feel about the community, then you’ll have a clearer picture of the ecosystem
        * Example from TTW: [https://github.com/orgs/the-turing-way/discussions/2644](https://github.com/orgs/the-turing-way/discussions/2644) 
    * People will always be members of multiple communities
        * Don’t need to try too hard to separate them
        * Separation might be needed if you have a funder commitment
    * Another example of how strategy has been developed: [https://github.com/alan-turing-institute/environment-and-sustainability-gc-community/blob/main/docs/strategy/community-strategy.md](https://github.com/alan-turing-institute/environment-and-sustainability-gc-community/blob/main/docs/strategy/community-strategy.md) 
    * Maybe we should develop 1-pager guidance on how to do this?!
        * Giving credit to the communities which inspire
* Reflection:
    * It’s common to be a part of multiple communities. There is no pressure to make explicit distinctions between them, especially with overlapping themes.
    * Start with establishing a clear purpose for the community and direct your focus on that. To develop this, best to reach out and speak to members of the community.
    * Get involved with and pick up from other similar communities doing this well.
